# The Toddler Kitchen voice — derived from the corpus, not invented

Derived 2026-09-01 from all 143 published posts (183,908 words) on the local
WP mirror (prod copy). **Corpus covers the site through 2026-07-13** (the
mirror's newest post) — posts published after that date are not yet in the
counts. Numbers below are corpus counts — this is what Alison actually does,
measured. To refresh: re-import the prod DB, re-run the corpus analysis, and
update this stamp; the shape rarely changes, the examples do.
Offline fallback exemplars: `../../kitchen-post/data/voice/`.

**Scope note:** this profile is the BLOG voice. Alison's Instagram caption
register (emoji habits, caption length, hashtags, CTA style) has not been

## Identity on the page

First person, warm, practical: a dietitian who is also a mom in the middle
of it. The credential line is **"As a dietitian"** (32 posts), often
**"As a dietitian and mom"** — almost never "pediatric dietitian" in prose
(1 use ever). "(By a Dietitian)" appears as a title parenthetical. Real-life
texture is common and specific: "my kids" (39 posts), "my daughter" (26),
honest mess admissions ("Did it get a bit messy? Yes.").

## Mechanics (measured, non-negotiable)

- **Canadian -our spellings, -ize endings**: flavour (337 vs 4), colour
  (129 vs 1), favourite (200 vs 1), fibre (334 vs 11), savoury — but
  customize, organize, practicing, and always "yogurt" (556 vs 0).
- **The dash is a spaced hyphen " - "** (764 uses). Em dashes are basically
  absent (28 in 184k words). Never introduce them.
- **No emoji** (39 chars across the whole corpus — effectively zero).
- **Exclamations: ~1 per 100 words.** Warm, not breathless — roughly one
  per paragraph at most, usually closing a benefit sentence.
- Sentences average 17 words; paragraphs are short (2-3 sentences).

## Vocabulary that is the brand

Heavy rotation: **"little one(s)"** (95/144 posts), **"your child"** (398
uses — beats "your toddler" 98), **"kid-friendly"** (65 posts),
"toddler-friendly", "no added sugar", "iron-rich", "perfect for",
"great option", "I love / we love" (100+ posts combined).
Rare to absent (do not introduce): "kiddo" (3), "mama" (7), clinical
jargon, and every banned list in `hook-writing-floor.md`.
Picky eating is named with empathy ("can really feel overwhelming"), never
with fix-it promises.

## The three proven openers

1. **Reader question**: "Need some ideas for lunches that you can prep
   ahead of time and freeze?"
2. **Benefits declarative**: "These Sheet Pan Black Bean Quesadillas are
   easy to make, cooked hands-off in the oven, and are the perfect
   kid-friendly lunch or dinner."
3. **Empathy first**: "Packing school lunches can feel like a lot at the
   best of times, and then if you add picky eating into the mix, it can
   really feel overwhelming."
Signature hybrid: "As a dietitian and mom, I get asked all the time
about…" — credential + relatability in one line.

## The post skeleton (presence across 143 posts)

Intro → affiliate disclosure → **"Why You'll Love This/These [Recipe]"**
(56) → Ingredients (108) with **"See recipe card for quantities."** (109) →
Instructions overview (61) → Top tip / Hint (31) → Substitutions (104) →
Variations (67) → Equipment (96) → Storage (101) → Toddler Tip or Nutrition
Tip (~78) → FAQ (115, three to five Q&As, answers 2-4 sentences) →
Related (106). Recipe posts get the WPRM card; roundups swap the middle for
the list. Deviate only when the content genuinely demands it.

## RD framing patterns

- **Division of responsibility**, named or paraphrased: parent provides,
  child decides what and how much.
- Self-feeding and skill practice as the reason behind textures and formats.
- **Safety as capability**: "finely ground for younger toddlers", "keep
  seeds and nuts finely ground until about the age of 4" — the prep is the
  content, the food is never the villain.
- Iron gets recurring emphasis ("iron-rich" 110 uses, often with
  "often iron-fortified!" for cereals).
- Nutrition claims are modest and specific ("a good plant-based source of
  iron and healthy fats") — never cures, never superfood talk.
