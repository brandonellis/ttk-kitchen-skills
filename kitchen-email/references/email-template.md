# Weekly email template and email voice

The fixed shape of every Sunday email. The section order is fixed. The sale
section appears only when a sale window applies (see
`seasonal-calendar.md`). For colours and Kit blocks, see `kit-layout.md`.

```
SUBJECT:  <one of 3 options, ≤60 characters>
PREVIEW:  <preview text, ≤90 characters, adds to the subject without repeating it>

Hi {{ subscriber.first_name }}!

[INTRO DRAFT - Ali to rewrite]
<2-4 short paragraphs: a seasonal or real-life opener, then a line
pointing at this week's recipes>

[SALE BOX - sale weeks only]
<heading> <2-4 sentences about the 3x5 Dinner Prep Method> <pink button>

<Recipe title>                          (heading)
<post image, left, text wraps around it>
<2-3 sentence blurb>
[ <Recipe name> ]                       (teal button)

<...1-2 more recipes...>

Have a great week!
Ali

[P.S. BOX]
P.S. <3x5 call to action, 1-3 sentences>
[ See the 3x5 Dinner Prep Method ]      (pink button)

<affiliate disclaimer, only if the email has an affiliate link>
<about-me bio, divider, social icons, footer: kept from her template>
```

## Email voice (measured from Kit, 2026-10-22)

Ali's emails are chattier than her blog. Measured from 13 sent subject
lines and 3 full emails, these patterns override the blog profile for email
only:

- **Greeting:** "Hi {{ subscriber.first_name }}!" every time.
- **Sign-off:** "Have a great week!" (sometimes with a seasonal lead-in,
  like "Enjoy these sunny days and have a great week!"), then "Ali".
- **Exclamations:** more than the blog, about one per paragraph. Still warm,
  never breathless.
- **Emoji:** at most one, at the start of a subject line or a section
  heading (5 of 13 subjects have one). None in paragraphs.
- **Openers:** seasonal and personal ("The crisp fall days are upon us and
  I am here for it!"), or a reader question ("Have any leftover
  zucchini?").
- **Blurbs:** "These [recipe] are..." or "This [recipe] is such a fun...",
  followed by what makes it easy and one nutrition or texture detail.
- **Button labels:** the recipe or post name, like "Watermelon Pizza".
- **Unchanged from the blog:** Canadian spelling (favourite, practise as a
  verb), spaced hyphen " - " and never an em dash, the RD framing, and the
  writing floor.

When newer emails show a different habit, the newest emails win. Note the
change in the handback so this section gets updated.

## Section rules

**Subject lines.** Write 3 options, each using a different catch from the
writing floor (curiosity gap, vivid specific, twist, claimed payoff). Keep
them ≤60 characters, use at most one emoji at the start, and no ALL CAPS.
Mark the one you recommend. Her subjects often name the recipe ("Need a
quick toddler snack? Mango Bliss Balls!"), which is a good default.

**Intro.** Ali rewrites this herself, so draft a starting point, not a
finished piece. Put a bold `[INTRO DRAFT - Ali to rewrite]` line above it.
Write it in her email voice so the rewrite is light. Never invent anecdotes
about her family or kids ("my oldest loved these!"). Her intros are full of
those, which is exactly why she writes them. Seasonal openers are safe:
"Pumpkin season is in full swing over here!"

**Recipe blocks.**
- The heading is the post's title (you can drop the parenthetical) and the
  button links to the real post URL.
- The blurb is 2-3 sentences: what it is, why it helps a parent this week,
  and one specific detail from the post (freezes well, 20 minutes,
  self-feeding friendly, iron-rich). Every fact comes from the post.
- Use the post's featured image (its `og:image`). If the post has none,
  leave the image out rather than using a placeholder.

**Sale box (sale weeks only).** This is info about the 3x5 Dinner Prep
Method, tied to the occasion ("Black Friday", "a fresh start for January
dinners", "back-to-school weeknights"). Discount, code and end date appear
**only if Ali gave them** in the run. Otherwise, describe the method and
link to it without any sale claim. Never invent a percentage or a deadline.

**P.S. box (every email).** One short call to action for the 3x5 Dinner
Prep Method, linked to the sales page. Rotate the angle week to week (see
the approved angles in `3x5-method.md`) so it doesn't read the same every
Sunday. In a sale week, the PS repeats the sale in one line instead.

**Affiliate disclaimer.** Include it only if the email has an affiliate
link. Copy the wording from her latest email exactly.

## Example P.S. lines (patterns to adapt, not copy)

- P.S. If 4pm is the hardest part of your day, my 3x5 Dinner Prep Method
  breaks dinner into three 5-minute steps you can do whenever you have a
  minute.
- P.S. One dinner for the whole family, with responsive feeding tips built
  into every recipe - that's my 3x5 Dinner Prep Method!

## Length

The body (greeting to P.S.) is 200-350 words without a sale box and up to
450 with one. Parents read this on their phone on a Sunday, so short beats
complete.
